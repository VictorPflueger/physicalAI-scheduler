## Part 1. What was asked and what was built

**The brief.** A new physical-AI prototype to show a client, drafted from an e-mail exchange about passenger information on the railway: how AI could keep the operational timetable (Betriebsfahrplan) and the customer timetable (Kundenfahrplan) better in step, on the condition that the AI must not become a second source of timetable truth but should connect operational reality with what passengers are told. The reply in that exchange proposed the concept built here: the AI as a **translator** that keeps no timetable of its own, traces every fact back to the shared source, derives passenger information from operational changes such as platform changes or diversions, and reports contradictions between the two timetables, in the spirit of the world model of the whitepaper *Two AI Worlds, One Robot*. Design (colour coding, features, run model) was to follow the two sister prototypes.

| Requirement | Where it lives | Status |
|---|---|---|
| No second source of timetable truth | The engine derives the customer view from the source (`js/sync.js`); the agent has no tool that writes a timetable; options with hand-set values are labelled "second truth" | Built |
| Every fact traceable to the source | Contradictions name the source version, the change, the channel version and the cause; the fact audit (`js/facts.js`) refuses any time, platform, minute figure, train or passenger count no tool returned; drafts carry a source reference | Built |
| Passenger information derived from operational changes | Platform, time, stop not served, stop missing, terminus, coach order and connection contradictions; passenger messages German first, then English | Built |
| Contradictions reported, humans decide | Verified alerts, three costed options plus doing nothing, approvals, an outbox connected to nothing | Built |
| Link to the whitepaper's world model | The physical layer (`phys_observe`) corrects and confirms the source; a verified alert needs it (`README.md`, *The concept*) | Built |
| Sister-app design and features | Part 2 | Built |
| Public repository names no client, contact or meeting | `tests/hygiene.mjs` | Built, checked on every push |

### Version 1.3.0: on Vici (28 October 2026)

**The request.** The Timetable Translator still had the sister apps' former look (a burgundy palette generated by tools/palette.mjs, Barlow and IBM Plex Mono, round shapes, its own icons and logo). It moves onto Vici, Victor's design system, so that it looks like the rest of his work, in a light and a dark theme, without changing what it does, the way Rebate Hunter moved in its 7.2.0 (`VictorPflueger/PROC-Rebate-Hunter`, commit `73c00f8`), whose shell it takes over.

**What changed** (`DESIGN.md`; `TECH_NOTES.md` D-T28 to D-T35):
- *Vici copied in unchanged* (`vici/`, release v1.0.0): its stylesheet, script, tokens and fonts, the byline's portrait and LinkedIn's logo. The former palette, its generator, css/tokens.css, design/tokens.json, css/fonts.css, fonts/ and images/author.jpg are gone. The shared stylesheets, `js/theme.js`, the shell modules and four tests are Rebate Hunter's, with this app's names and comments.
- *Colours by meaning, on Vici's tokens:* merlot is the human in charge, lilac the AI (the translator, a what-if, the first estimate), danger, warning and success the state of passenger information, fjord checking, neutral ink the timetables and the network at rest. A selection on the mirror is a 2 px ink frame, because merlot sits close to danger. Every state also has a form: a word, a mark on every card and line label, a dashed ring or edge, an underline. The simulation's tape is neutral, always with the word SIMULATION.
- *Hexagons instead of circles:* the corridor's stations, rings, pulses, sensor marks and train, the spine's stops, the lines' end marks, the alignment view's numbers, the pips, badges, steps and spinners; capsules instead of pills (line labels, channel chips, tags); the canvas's honeycomb.
- *Vici's components, icons and type:* buttons, tags, stats, cards, dialogs, tabs, the stepper, tables and alerts; Vici's icons, with twelve drawn in its style where it has none (Rebate Hunter's eight and four of this app's own, `DESIGN.md` §11); Bricolage Grotesque, Inter and JetBrains Mono. The sizes the room needs stay (`DESIGN.md` §10).
- *The page icon* is the prototype's card on Victor's website (Vici's clock icon in a lilac hexagon cell): header, loading screen and favicon. *Vici's byline* replaces the credit in the drawer bar.
- *A dark theme* beside the light default, with Vici's toggle in the header.

**What stayed.** Features, the agent's flow, data, figures, texts, both modes and the keyboard paths. The behaviour tests check the same things as before; their selectors follow Vici's class names.

**How it was checked.** Every suite passes (`TECH_NOTES.md` §11). New: the colour test on Vici's values in both themes (behind the old `tokens` id), the hexagon test, the page-icon test, the check of `vici/`, and the smoke cases *byline* and *once* (every control does its job exactly once, since vici.js acts on some controls by itself). The design suite runs in both themes, and screenshots at 1920 × 1080 and 1280 × 720 in both themes were compared with the same views before the change and with Rebate Hunter at `73c00f8`. Every view was also compared with 1.2.0 text by text (whatever an ellipsis cut, at five sizes and in every scenario) and by the alignment view's size in every state: Inter runs about a fifth wider than the former condensed figures, and where it cut or stepped down what 1.2.0 showed whole, the room went to it (`TECH_NOTES.md` §11).

## Part 3. Findings

| ID | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | A passenger was counted once per wrong fact: the 104 seat reservations on ICE 618 were added to the 312 passengers boarding (416), and the narration said 312 board | Counted once per stop (`distinctPax`), a golden test and a world check that the groups nest |
| T2 | The engine wrote a normalised train id back into the model's own tool input, which is echoed to the API and recorded | The engine works on copies |
| T3 | A time before a comma or full stop ("07:44,") escaped the fact audit | The pattern ends at a digit boundary; tenths ("08:37.6") stay excluded |

Found by the independent review (an agent that had not built the prototype, 18 October 2026) and fixed:

| ID | Finding | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | The hygiene check kept its term list in the repository, encoded but readable | The list lives outside the repository (a CI secret, or a file next to the folder) |
| R2 | The fact audit accepted a value any tool had returned, for any train, and read no coach sectors; a passenger message could name the old platform as if it were current | Facts by train, sectors audited, an outdated value only beside the source's value |

## Part 5. The customer's view: a five-to-ten-minute pitch

**The request.** Version 1.0.0 was complete, but too much to explain in a five-minute call. The rework had to let a customer understand, in five to ten minutes, what the prototype does, what it achieves and how the AI aligns the information, without leaving the sister apps' design.

**Method.** The pitch was watched from the customer's seat: the first screen, a run at ×1 without explanation, the decision and what it leaves, at 1920 × 1080, 1600 × 900 and 1280 × 720 (a projector), and every figure on screen compared with the engine's. Each row below is something a customer would not have understood, or would have read wrongly, and what changed. `tests/pitch.mjs` checks the result as the audience sees it; `DEMO_SCRIPT.md` is the stage script for five and for ten minutes.
