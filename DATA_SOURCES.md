# Timetable Translator — data sources

What the Timetable Translator reads, what is real and what is invented, and every assumption the sync engine makes. World and engine version 1.0.0, 18 October 2026, unchanged in app versions 1.1.0 to 1.3.0.

**In one paragraph.** The stations, their Ril 100 codes and the lines between them are real public facts. Everything that happens on them is invented: every train number, time, track, passenger figure, operational change, publication state, sensor reading and connection. No railway's data, systems or rules are used, and the publication rules are assumptions written for this demonstration. The only outside service the app talks to is the Anthropic API, for the live agent.

## 2. Geography: stations, Ril 100 codes and lines (real)

The corridor runs from Köln to Dortmund along two real routes, with a third real line joining them:

| Line in the app | Stations (Ril 100 code) |
|---|---|
| Main line | Köln Hbf (KK) – Köln Messe/Deutz (KKDZ) – Köln-Mülheim (KKM) – Leverkusen Mitte (KLMI) – Düsseldorf Hbf (KD) – Düsseldorf Flughafen (KDFF) – Duisburg Hbf (EDG) – Mülheim (Ruhr) Hbf (EMLR) – Essen Hbf (EE) – Bochum Hbf (EBO) – Dortmund Hbf (EDO) |
| Wuppertal line | Köln-Mülheim (KKM) – Solingen Hbf (KSO) – Wuppertal Hbf (KW) – Wuppertal-Oberbarmen (KWO) – Hagen Hbf (EHG) – Witten Hbf (EWIT) – Dortmund Hbf (EDO) |
| Link | Düsseldorf Hbf (KD) – Wuppertal Hbf (KW) |

- **Codes and names.** All 16 codes and station names were checked, code by code, against the public directory of operating points (Betriebsstellenverzeichnis, Ril 100, formerly DS 100; open-data edition of April 2018), as packaged in the `ds-100` module (github.com/fourplusone/ds100): every code exists and names the station the app gives it. The directory is not redistributed here; the app uses the 16 codes and names only.
- **Lines.** The three lines are real routes between these stations. The app draws them as a schematic (`js/mirror_strip.js`): positions are spaced for legibility, not to scale, and the lines are simplified to the stations above.
- **Platforms.** The number of platform tracks per station and their lengths (405 m unless stated) are plausible but **invented**; so is every track a train uses. The station popovers say so.
- **Places named in records.** A few records name real places beyond the stations, such as a depot in Köln or an interlocking at Wuppertal-Oberbarmen. The places are real; every record about them is invented.

## 3. The synthetic operating snapshot (invented)

`data/world.js` is generated by `tools/build_world.mjs` and checked by `tools/check_world.mjs` (41 checks: every trip runs on the network, forwards in time, in tenths of a minute, on existing tracks; every operational change has a record and is corroborated by the physical layer; publication states name existing versions and causes; seat reservations and passengers who change are within their stop's counts; no channel is ahead of the source; every scenario shows the contradictions it is about while every other train matches the source; every reference case and every simulation script passes the live guardrails).

- **The moment.** One morning, frozen at **07:28**. Every minute figure counts from there; the planning horizon is **180 minutes**.
- **Trains: 82.** 9 ICE, 18 RE, 46 S-Bahn runs and 9 replacement buses, with invented numbers (ICE 618, RE 10109, RB 30471, S 30124, Bus SEV 1 …) and invented source timetables: every operating point with arrival, departure or passing time to a tenth of a minute, track, activity (passenger stop, operational stop, pass) and remarks; the ICE also carry a coach order. The line names (RE 1, RE 4, RE 7, RE 57, RB 48, S 11) are used as everyday labels, not as a statement about who runs them.
- **Operational changes: 5**, in the operations log with their record text in operating language: OPS-01 a set turned in the depot (reversed coach order, ICE 516), OPS-02 a construction order with a speed restriction Bochum – Dortmund (+4.0 min, ICE 612 and RE 10109), OPS-03 an interlocking failure at Wuppertal-Oberbarmen with two ICE diverted via Essen and Düsseldorf and replacement buses Hagen – Wuppertal, OPS-04 a train ending at Köln Messe/Deutz, OPS-05 a short-notice platform change at Köln Hbf. Each change creates a new source version of the trains it touches.
- **Published channels.** For every train and data channel (journey planner and app, station displays, announcements, coach order), the source version it was generated from, when, and why it lags: one of seven invented causes (*short-notice change*, *rejected by the plausibility check*, *conversion rule fault*, *journey-planner import queued*, *on-board announcements on the old run*, *coach order is a separate feed*, *construction order after the daily run*).
- **Physical layer: 9 observations**: point diagnostics and a stored route at Köln Hbf, a stored route with the end of a run at Köln Messe/Deutz, an interlocking failure at Wuppertal-Oberbarmen, a train position approaching Dortmund, door logs of the last five runs and the platform length at Köln-Mülheim, an RFID coach-order reading at the depot exit, measured section times Bochum – Dortmund under the speed restriction. Each says which change or timetable fact it corroborates.
- **Passengers.** Boarding, alighting and seat reservations per train and stop, and one connection (ICE 612 → RE 57 in Dortmund, 4-minute transfer, 46 passengers). All invented, sized to look plausible for a weekday morning.

## 4. Model assumptions (deterministic sync engine)

The engine (`js/sync.js`) derives what each customer channel should show from the source and compares it with what the channel published. Its rules are written for the demonstration; none is a railway's actual rule.

- **Customer times.** Passengers see whole minutes: arrivals rounded **up**, departures rounded **down**, so a customer time never promises more than the source (`js/world.js`). Operational stops (Betriebshalt) and passes are not published. A short stop keeps arrival ≤ departure.
- **Contradictions.** Seven types: *platform*, *time* (one minute or more), *stop not served*, *stop missing*, *terminus*, *coach order* (first class and the bistro in other sectors), *connection* (a transfer shown as reachable that no longer is).
- **Who relies on a contradiction.** The passengers boarding or alighting at the stop concerned (for a platform or a coach order, those boarding; for a terminus, those on board for the old destination; for a connection, those who change). Passengers travelling to a stop count only if they are on board when they act: those who planned to board at a stop a diverted train no longer serves are counted where they wanted to board, not again on board. The snapshot has counts per stop, not journeys, so the share on board is taken in proportion to the boardings (of the 247 travelling to Köln Hbf on ICE 1022, the share that boards at Dortmund: 186 of the 356 boarding before Köln on the published run).
- **Counting passengers once.** Counts are per stop: those boarding there, and those travelling to it. Someone who relies on two wrong facts at the same stop counts once there: the passengers with seat reservations are among those boarding (platform and coach sectors), those who change are among those alighting (arrival time and connection). They count as misinformed if at least one of the two facts misleads them, the two taken as independent. One case is still counted twice, and none of the six scenarios has it: a passenger with a wrong departure where they board and a wrong arrival where they alight (a what-if that delays a train everywhere).
- **Who would act on wrong information.** Each group plans evenly over a window before its event and can act on new information until the window's end, the last moment (platform 20 to 2 minutes before departure, coach order 15 to 1, stop not served 40 to 5, stop missing 30 to 3, terminus 40 to 3, time and connection 30 to 3). The journey planner is read when people plan: once corrected, it reaches its share of those who plan after the correction. Every other channel reaches people in real time, up to the last moment, including those already on the platform or on board: the app push, the station displays, the announcements, staff and crew, the coach-position displays. A real-time correction reaches its full share if it is in place at least 5 minutes before the last moment, proportionally less if later. Shares: journey planner 55 % of those boarding and 35 % of those on board, displays 90 % / 10 %, announcements 75 % / 85 %, coach order 80 % / 0 %, app push 35 % / 45 %, staff 30 % / 80 %. A passenger no channel reaches in time acts on the wrong information. The expected count is rounded to whole passengers. *First affected in* on the tiles is the first departure or arrival a wrong fact concerns, not the moment the first passenger acts.
- **Publication.** A daily generation at 05:30 and a half-hourly cycle at :00 and :30 that closes 15 minutes before it runs: at 07:28 the 07:30 cycle has closed, so the next cycle a change can still take is **08:00**. The cycle does not take a short-notice change, a rejected update, a conversion fault, the on-board announcements, the coach-order feed or a construction order released after the daily run; it does take a queued journey-planner import. A channel that stays wrong is "not back in sync within 180 minutes".
- **Responses and their timings.** Re-publishing from the source takes 6 minutes (12 more where a rejected update must first be checked and released by hand), with the displays 2 minutes behind the journey planner; a passenger message reaches the app in 2 minutes, displays and announcements in 3, the journey planner in 4; a manual override takes 2 minutes; staff are in place after 5. Staff effort: re-publication 3 minutes (+6 for releasing a rejected update), 2 per message channel, 2 per override value plus 2 to remove it later, 4 for a staff briefing, 3 for a hold request.
- **What each option is scored on.** Passengers still misinformed (the headline figure), when the right information first reaches passengers, when every channel is back in sync with the source, how many values are kept by hand (a second truth), and staff effort. A hold request keeps the connection for those who change if dispatch agrees, and delays the other passengers boarding the connecting train there by the wait the engine computes; dispatch decides.
- **Scope.** A result is *from the source* only when it covers every train the same operational change leaves wrong, every channel and every stop; otherwise it is *partial* and names what it leaves out. A *what-if* applies hypothetical changes to a copy of the source and is labelled as such. Options may rest only on a result from the source.

## 5. The six scenarios: what is real

| Scenario | Real | Invented |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Platform change at Köln Hbf | The station and its platform tracks as places | The points failure, the change of ICE 618 to Gleis 7, every channel's lag, all figures |
| 2 · Diversion via Essen | The two routes between Dortmund and Köln, the stations | The interlocking failure, the diversion of ICE 1022 and ICE 1026, the rejected update, the replacement buses, all figures |
| 3 · Operational stop as a passenger stop | Köln-Mülheim as a station | ICE 1027's operational stop, the conversion fault, the door logs, the platform length, all figures |
| 4 · New terminus Köln Messe/Deutz | Köln Messe/Deutz and Köln Hbf | ICE 1025's early end, the queued import, the on-board announcements, all figures |
| 5 · Reversed coach order | The depot in Köln as a place | The turned set of ICE 516, the RFID reading, the coach-order feed, all figures |
| 6 · Construction order in Dortmund | Bochum Hbf and Dortmund Hbf | The speed restriction, the construction order, the connection to RE 57, all figures |

The kinds of failure are realistic for any railway's passenger information (short-notice changes, plausibility checks, conversion rules, separate feeds, daily generation runs); their details and frequency here are not taken from any operator.

**Evidence in Simulation mode.** The scripts replay a representative run: every lookup is re-run against the world model at playback, every figure is recomputed by the engine, and the narration was written to match. Nothing in a simulated run was retrieved from outside.

## 6. Caveats to mention when presenting

- The morning is fictional; the stations and lines are real. Say it once: *"The stations are real; the trains and figures are fictional."*
- The passenger counts are expected values from stated assumptions (§4), not forecasts.
- The physical layer is simulated. In a real deployment it would be the operator's interlocking, positioning and vehicle data, behind its own access rules.
- The app publishes nothing and is connected to no system: approving a draft only marks it as cleared.
- The live agent's text is generated by Claude and checked in code for timetable facts; its wording is not checked.

## 7. What the Timetable Translator does **not** use

- No railway's timetable data, published channels, interfaces or business rules.
- No web search, no open-data APIs, no maps, no analytics, no fonts or scripts loaded from a third party. The only front-end assets are Victor Pflüger's design system Vici, copied into `vici/` (`vici/README.md`): its stylesheet, script and tokens (MIT), its fonts Bricolage Grotesque, Inter and JetBrains Mono (SIL Open Font License 1.1), his portrait for the byline and LinkedIn's own logo file, all served from the app itself.
- No personal data: drafts address teams by function, and the agent is told not to write names, phone numbers or e-mail addresses.
