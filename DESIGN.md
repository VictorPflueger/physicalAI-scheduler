# Timetable Translator — design system

How the Timetable Translator looks, why it looks that way, and how to change it without breaking it. Version 1.4.0, 29 October 2026.

The Timetable Translator is built on **Vici**, Victor Pflüger's design system (release v1.0.0, copied unchanged into `vici/`; `vici/README.md` says which files and from where). Vici decides the look: its tokens in a light and a dark theme, its fonts, components, icons and hexagon shapes. This prototype's rules decide the meaning: what a colour stands for, what needs emphasis, what the room must see. Where the two meet, this file says how, and §10 lists every deliberate deviation from Vici with its reason.

The Timetable Translator shares its shell with its sister prototypes, the **Navigator** (supply-chain disruptions), **Rebate Hunter** (procurement rebates) and **Repack Pilot**: the same Vici copy, the same shell code and styles, the same colour grammar and agentic patterns. The page icon, the names and the domain words differ, and so does the centrepiece: where the Navigator shows a map and Rebate Hunter a board, the Timetable Translator shows a **timetable mirror**, built from the same canvas, floating controls, legend card, popovers and colour grammar (§3). `REVIEW.md` lists the parity item by item, and `tests/parity.mjs` checks the shared shell against Rebate Hunter's file by file.

---

## 1. The brief

- **On Vici.** The prototype looks like the rest of Victor's work: merlot ink and pastel surfaces, Bricolage Grotesque, Inter and JetBrains Mono, hexagons where other systems draw circles. Its page icon is the card it has on Victor's website (§4).
- **Legible in 60 seconds.** A partner who has never seen the tool should get the point from across a room, on a 1080p projector, often in a lit room: *this train leaves from another platform than every channel says, this many passengers rely on the wrong one, the first of them in fourteen minutes, and this is the cleanest way to put it right.*
- **Agentic.** An AI agent acts on screen. At every moment the viewer must be able to tell who is acting: the agent, or the person who has to decide.
- **A translator, not a second source.** The screen must show that the customer timetable is derived from the operational one: two timetables side by side, the translation between them drawn as lines, every published fact traceable to its source row.

Everything below follows from those four lines.

## 2. Methods, and what each one decided

| Method | Source | What it decided |
|---|---|---|
| A design system with semantic tokens | Vici v1.0.0 (`vici/`) | Every colour is one of Vici's semantic tokens (`--vc-text`, `--vc-accent`, `--vc-danger-solid` …), which switch with the theme; no colour values of the app's own (§4) |
| The hexagon rule | Vici, *Hexagon* guideline: "If it would be round, make it a hexagon" | Stations, rings, stops, markers, pulses, pips, badges and pills are flat-top hexagons and hex capsules (§4); `tests/hexagon.mjs` keeps it so |
| Colour-blind separation | Machado, Oliveira & Fernandes (2009) | The state colours are measured under simulated colour blindness (`tests/tokens.mjs`); where Vici's tones come close, form carries the difference (§3, §5) |
| Accessibility | WCAG 2.2 | Contrast for every pairing in both themes, the focus ring, target size, no meaning by colour alone |
| Agentic UX patterns | Yocco, *Designing For Agentic AI*, Smashing Magazine, Feb 2026 | The progress rail (intent preview), the handover to the human, the audit trail |
| AI labelling | IBM Carbon for AI; Pickering, *Tooltips & Toggletips* | An **AI** label (Vici's feature tag, the sparkle and "AI") on the options, drafts and summary the agent wrote, with a short explanation one click away |
| Emphasis | Google, *Expressive Design: Google's UX Research* (Material 3 Expressive) | One emphasised action per moment: colour, size and containment go to the step that needs the person |
| Projection | VUMC Edge for Scholars, *Optimizing Colors for Projected Presentations* | Pastels never carry meaning alone; every pastel fill has a deeper stroke or text step. Layouts are reviewed under a simulated washout |
| Dashboard patterns | Bach et al., *Dashboard Design Patterns* (2022) | Each KPI tile pairs one value (data information) with a line of context (meta information) |

## 3. Colour grammar

Each colour means one thing, everywhere.

| Colour (Vici's tokens) | Means | Where you see it | Never used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Merlot** (`--vc-accent` and its tokens) | **The human in charge** | Primary buttons; every Approve; the progress step that waits for you (*Your decision*, *Your approval*); the recommended option's frame (2 px of `--vc-accent` on `--vc-accent-soft`); the **You decide** card on the alignment view; the recommendation the agent hands to you and your decisions in the console and the audit; the number of the scenario **Start agent** will run; the focus ring; the LIVE badge | A mark among state colours: merlot sits close to Vici's danger tone (§5); a selection on the mirror, which is ink |
| **Danger, warning, success** (`--vc-danger-*`, `--vc-warning-*`, `--vc-success-*`) | **The state of passenger information**: contradicts the source (affected), informed but the data still behind or the risk accepted (at risk), re-publication from the source approved (resolved) | The mirror's channel cards, published rows, lines, line labels, corridor rings, channel chips and monitor rows; KPI tiles; status tags; the comparison bars; *Confirmed* and *Not confirmed* in the console | Decoration, and buttons other than the two destructive ones |
| **Fjord** (`--vc-info-*`) | Checking | The column, station, channel or line the agent is reading (a pulse), the tags of what a lookup confirmed (*Source read*, *Physical layer read*, *Published view read*, *Passengers read*, *Read by the AI*, *Confirms the source*), the station a sensor was read at, *Check* steps in the console, links to sources, the *Verified against the source* tag | |
| **Lilac** (`--vc-feature-*`) | **The AI's voice** | AI tags (`vc-tag--feature` with the sparkle and "AI"), *Hypothesis* and *Revised* lines, the translator between the two timetables (*derived*) and the AI translator's card, what-if alerts (`vc-alert--feature`) and what-if contradictions on the mirror, the *First estimate* tag, the reasoning summaries, the streaming cursor, model calls in the audit, the AI disclosure | Anything a person decided |
| **Ink** (`--vc-text`, Vici's neutrals) | Everything else: the agent's work and the timetables at rest; the network at rest (`--vc-neutral-solid`) | Text, tool calls, separators, neutral controls, Reject, guardrail notes; the source card's 2 px frame (the one truth); the timetable rows, their stops and the lines between them at rest; the route today (ink) and as published (neutral, dashed), the network's lines | |
| **The tape** (`--vc-text` and `--vc-surface` stripes) | **Simulation** | The SIMULATION badge, the tape and the watermark, always with the word SIMULATION. Outside every state colour (Vici's honey now means *at risk*), so a replay can never pass for a live run | |

On stage, in one sentence: *"Merlot is you, lilac is the AI, red, honey and green are what passengers are told."*

**Every state has a form of its own** (WCAG 1.4.1), because Vici's state tones are close in lightness and some come close under colour blindness (§5): a word (*Contradicts the source*, *Data still wrong*, *Re-publication approved*); a mark on every channel card and line label (Vici's warning triangle, a clock, a hand, a check, the sparkle for a what-if); a dashed ring or edge (a what-if, a stop only the published timetable still has, a channel behind the source); an underline on the wrong value. The danger tone appears on two buttons only, **Abort** and **Forget key**, as an outline, because they stop or delete something.

**The timetable mirror in this grammar.** The mirror has three views, and all speak the same colours.
- *Alignment* (the first view: the pitch is told on it). Three columns of cards on the canvas (Vici's sunken background with its honeycomb), numbered in hexagons like the steps of a story. **1 The source**: the operational timetable at one stop of the train (the train itself is the view's caption), with a 2 px ink frame (the one truth), its values as large figures (*Platform 7*, *1st class F–G*), when and by whom it changed (the change's id only in the tooltip), and a card for the sensors of the physical layer, which shows the one reading that bears most on the train (*Interlocking: route out of platform 7 stored*, the others in its tooltip) once the agent has read it, tagged in fjord while it reads. **2 The translator**: a lilac card (the AI's voice) with a few words of what the AI is doing now (*3 options proposed*, *2 drafts ready*) and its one rule, *Keeps no timetable of its own*, and below it the **You decide** card, solid merlot when a decision or an approval waits for the person, soft merlot with a 2 px merlot frame once decided, honey with the hand when the risk is accepted. **3 What passengers are told**: one card per channel with its value and its verdict (their common age, *as of 05:30*, stands once at the column's head; a channel of another age carries its own), the state carried as a bar on the card's left edge in the state token, a tint, a mark and a word: *Not checked yet* (ink), *Reading…* (fjord), *Read, not compared yet* (ink: the AI has read the channel, the engine has not compared it; a verdict and its colour come only from the engine), *Contradicts the source* (red, warning mark), *Matches the source*, then after the decision *Re-publication approved* (green, check mark, the old value struck through and the source's value after it; approved, not published, as nothing is connected), *Data still wrong* (honey, clock), *Override approved: a second truth* (honey, hand), *Wrong: risk accepted* (honey, hand). Arrows between the columns run dashed fjord while the AI reads and turn green or honey with the decision. Under the columns, the **impact line** gives the engine's figure in large type with when the first passenger acts (where, in its tooltip), and after the decision what is left in two clauses, who is still misinformed and whether the data is aligned; it stays red while passengers act on wrong information. The view sizes itself by its own height: on a projector or large screen the figures grow; on a laptop the sub-heads go; with the drawer open the channels sit two by two, then in one row with the verdict as a mark in the card's corner, so the finding is in view at every step of a run. A card is a button: a channel opens its own timetable, the source opens the timetables.
- *Timetables.* At the top, the **corridor strip**: the main line Köln – Düsseldorf – Duisburg – Essen – Bochum – Dortmund along the bottom, the line through Solingen, Wuppertal and Hagen along the top, each station with its Ril 100 code (and on a tall strip its short name; neighbouring names keep 10 px apart, a name moving a few pixels aside rather than run into the next). For a train that runs abroad, and only for it, the strip adds the cross-border line on the left, Köln – Aachen – Liège – Bruxelles, and the corridor moves right to make room; the border point is a dashed upright under its hexagon, the two countries' codes at its foot (BE on the west, DE on the east), a form rather than a colour. The train's route today, from the source, is a solid ink line; where the published route differs (a diversion the channels have not taken up) it is dashed in neutral. Passenger stops are hexagon rings, operational stops (Betriebshalt, no passengers) squares, passes small hexagons, a stop only the published timetable still has a dashed ring. A hexagon marker with an arrow shows where the train is at 07:28. A station of a contradiction gets a hexagon ring in its state colour (dashed for a what-if), a station the physical layer was read at a small fjord hexagon, and a station the agent is reading pulses in fjord. Below, the **mirror card**: on the left the Betriebsfahrplan (every operating point, arrival over departure in tenths of a minute, the Ril 100 code, the track, what changed against the base timetable), on the right the Kundenfahrplan of one channel (passenger stops only, whole minutes, platforms, or coach sectors for the coach order), each column with the version of the source it was generated from; a channel generated from an older version has a chip with a dashed edge. The channel shown has a 2 px ink edge on its chip. Between the columns, the translator: a line from every published row to its source row, so the customer row sits level with the row it was derived from. A line that ends in a hexagon with × is published but no longer in the source; one that ends in a hexagon with + is a passenger stop of the source the channel does not list. Each row's stop on the timetable's spine is a hexagon with an ink ring (the first and the last stop solid, a passing point small, an operational stop a square). When the engine finds a contradiction, the published row takes a bar and a tint in the state colour, the wrong value is underlined in the state's ink, the line turns the state colour and carries a label (a hex capsule) with the contradiction and the passengers who rely on it, and the source row it refers to gets a bar on its edge toward the line. After a decision the mirror follows it: what an approved option re-publishes turns green, what it only announces turns honey (the data is still behind), and an accepted risk stays visible in honey with a hand on its labels. Green means *re-publication approved*: nothing is published by the app.
- *All trains* (the sync monitor). Every train in the window, first departure first: its run in the corridor, its source version and the change that created it, the channels still behind the source (a tag, a fact of the data, not yet a finding), and what the agent's check found (*N contradictions · P pax* in red, *In sync with the source* in green, a decision in its state colour). A version behind turns red only when the engine says it contradicts what passengers need. The train in focus has a 2 px ink frame (`aria-current`), never merlot.

A selection on the mirror is always ink: the view shown (a 2 px ink frame on its button), the channel shown, the train in focus, the legend's ticked toggles.

**Why a mirror and not a map.** For a supply-chain disruption, place is the question; for a missed rebate, group membership. For passenger information the question is *correspondence*: does each fact passengers see correspond to a fact of the source? The mirror puts exactly that on screen, so the finding reads from across the room: *the source says platform 7, every channel says platform 5, 312 passengers rely on it.* The alignment view says it in one picture for a five-minute pitch (one stop, every channel, the AI and the person in between); the timetables view says it row against row for the expert; the strip keeps the geography that matters (which line the train really runs on) without making it the centrepiece.

## 4. Where the look comes from

**Colours.** Every colour is one of Vici's semantic tokens, which switch with the theme. The app has no colour values of its own; the favicon is the one exception, because a favicon cannot read CSS. Vici's family primitives (`--vc-lilac-100` and so on) appear only where Vici uses them itself (the page icon's lilac cell). JavaScript draws no colour: the mirror's SVG (the corridor strip, the lines between the timetables, the legend's swatches) takes its colours from classes in `css/mirror.css` and `css/align.css`, so a theme change repaints it, and the mirror still redraws on Vici's `vc:themechange` event. The roles of the former palette map onto Vici like this:

| Former role | Vici's token |
|---|---|
| `--paper` | `--vc-surface` |
| `--porcelain`, `--porcelain-2` | `--vc-bg`; `--vc-bg-subtle` for tracks, `--vc-surface-hover` for hover |
| `--line`, `--line-strong`, `--border-control` | `--vc-border-subtle`, `--vc-border`, `--vc-border-strong` |
| `--text`, `--text-muted`, `--text-faint` | `--vc-text`, `--vc-text-muted`, `--vc-text-subtle` |
| `--on-brand`, `--brand`, `--brand-deep` | `--vc-on-accent`; `--vc-accent`, `--vc-accent-hover`, `--vc-accent-pressed` |
| `--brand-text`, `--brand-mid`, `--brand-line` | `--vc-accent-text` (merlot text and icons, never among state colours); `--vc-accent-soft-hover` for quiet lines |
| `--brand-soft`, `--brand-wash` | `--vc-accent-soft` (`--vc-surface-selected` for a selected item in a list or menu), `--vc-surface-hover` |
| danger, warning, success, info (fill, mid, line, text) | `--vc-danger-bg`, `-border`, `-solid`, `-text` and the same for success and info; warning uses `--vc-warning-text` for lines too (its solid is a light fill, too pale for a stroke) |
| violet | lilac, Vici's feature tone: `--vc-feature-bg`, `-border`, `-solid`, `-text` |
| `--affected`, `--at-risk`, `--resolved` and their `-fill` variants | the line and mid tokens of danger, warning and success |
| `--network` | `--vc-neutral-solid`, for marks and lines alike (the lighter neutrals are too faint for lines that carry information) |
| `--live` | `--vc-accent` |
| the hazard yellow and black of the simulation | the neutral tape: `--vc-text` and `--vc-surface`, with `--vc-bg-subtle` as its wash |
| `--sea` (the mirror's canvas) | `--vc-bg-sunken`, with Vici's honeycomb pattern |
| `--r-sm`, `--r-md`, `--r-lg` | `--vc-radius-md`, `--vc-radius-lg`, `--vc-radius-xl` |
| `--shadow-pop`, `--shadow-soft` | `--vc-shadow-lg`, `--vc-shadow-md` |
| `--focus` | Vici's focus ring, `var(--vc-stroke-focus) solid var(--vc-focus)`, hexagonal on hexagon controls |

The app keeps a handful of tokens of its own in `css/style.css`: the type sizes Vici has no step for (`--fs-xs`, `--fs-lg`, `--fs-xl`, `--fs-kpi`; `--fs-sm` and `--fs-md` became Vici's `--vc-font-size-sm` and `--vc-font-size-md`), because the layout and the projector checks are tuned to them, and `--hit`, 44 px, the smallest target for what the room clicks.

**Themes.** Light is the default: the prototypes are mostly shown on projectors in lit rooms. `js/theme.js` sets `data-theme="dark"` only if the visitor chose dark with the header's toggle (Vici's toggle, moon and sun, tooltip *Dark theme*), `data-theme="light"` otherwise; Vici remembers the choice under `vc-theme`, the key all of Victor's pages share. Every contrast check runs in both themes (§5).

**Type.** Bricolage Grotesque (`--vc-font-display`) only for short display text: the app's name, the KPI figures, dialog and panel titles, and on the mirror its caption, the alignment view's column heads, the timetables' titles and the popovers' titles. Inter (`--vc-font-text`) for all other text, with tabular figures wherever numbers line up. JetBrains Mono (`--vc-font-mono`) for ids, timestamps, codes, tool names and raw output, and on the mirror for the Ril 100 codes, the timetables' times and the versions. Sentence case for headings, buttons, tabs and labels, except names, codes and the mode markings (§10). Reading text runs at most 75 characters per line: console entries, dialog texts, the summary, the drafts and the mirror's popovers take Vici's prose measure (`--vc-container-prose`, 50ch, about 65 characters of Inter), while tool calls and their raw results keep the console's width. The root size follows the projector, in relative units so it still follows the reader's browser setting: `clamp(93.75%, 0.48vw + 50%, 112.5%)`, 15 px at 1280 px wide and about 17 px at 1920 px.

**Shapes.** Anything round is a flat-top hexagon, anything pill-shaped a hex capsule with pointed ends (Vici's `--vc-hex`, `vc-hex`, `vc-capsule`, `vc-cell`): status and live pips, count badges, the status, mode and AI-disclosure tags, the byline's portrait, spinners and pulses, the intro's numbered steps, the rail's markers, the option letters, the menu's scenario numbers; on the mirror the corridor's stations, their state rings and marks, the pulse of a station being read, the sensor marks and the train's marker, the stops on the timetables' spines, the lines' end marks, the line labels and the channel chips (hex capsules), the alignment view's column numbers, the legend's swatches and toggles, and the canvas's honeycomb (Vici's pattern instead of a dot grid). The corridor strip and the end marks are drawn in SVG (`hexPoints` and `hexShape` in `js/mirror_strip.js`), so their outlines can be dashed. Bars have square ends. Rectangles keep small radii, up to `--vc-radius-xl`, and the smaller the box the smaller its radius (Vici: larger containers get larger radii, and nothing is fully rounded): the alignment view's cards step down from `--vc-radius-xl` to `--vc-radius-lg` and `--vc-radius-md` as the view condenses, and its value boxes keep `--vc-radius-md` or less, so nothing a line high reads as a pill. A dashed capsule (a channel generated from an older version) has its dashes along its outline, points and corners included. Other brands' logos stay as their owners publish them (LinkedIn's in the byline). In forced colours (Windows contrast themes) the app's own hexagons, capsules and links keep a visible outline, as Vici's cells do; a pressed control (the replay speed, the mirror's view and its legend) is drawn in Highlight, as Vici draws its own, and a timetable row's state bar becomes a border.

**Icons.** Vici's icons (`<span class="vc-icon vc-icon--NAME">`), through `js/icons.js`, which maps the app's names to Vici's (`reset` → `refresh`, `gear` → `settings`, `globe` → `globe-hex`, `spark` → `sparkle` …). Twelve things have no Vici icon yet: eight of them Rebate Hunter draws, and the app uses its drawings exactly, so a concept looks the same in every prototype; four are the Timetable Translator's own. The app draws them in Vici's style in the same module (24 × 24 with 2 px to spare, 1.75 px strokes with round caps and joins, hexagons where a shape would be round, chamfered corners, arrowheads that open at 120°) and proposes them for Vici (§11). They are written the way Vici writes its own icons, as masks (black strokes on the grid, which only say where the icon is), set with `element.style.setProperty()`, so they too take the colour of the text around them. Icon-only buttons have Vici's tooltip with their name. Two buttons that do different things never share an icon: *How it works* has Vici's *info*, the mirror's legend Vici's *layers* (its toggles are the mirror's layers), which tell them apart where their words hide.

**The page icon.** The prototype's mark is the card it has on Victor's website (data/prototypes.js in VictorPflueger/VIP: icon `clock`, kind *prototype*): Vici's clock icon in a lilac hexagon cell, in the header (`vc-cell vc-cell--lilac`), on the loading screen (`vc-cell--xl`) and, drawn larger and bolder so it reads at 16 px, as `favicon.svg`. If the website's card changes, the page icon changes with it, and the other way round (`tests/page-icon.mjs`).

**The byline.** The drawer bar carries Vici's byline: *Built by*, Victor's hexagon portrait and name, LinkedIn's own logo and a globe for his website, each a 44 px link that opens in a new tab. The app links to its author, never to its own source code.

## 5. The checks

`node tests/tokens.mjs` checks the colour system on Vici's values (`vici/dist/tokens.json`) in the light and the dark theme. It is the first suite in `node tests/run-all.mjs`.

**Contrast (WCAG 2.2).** Every text-on-fill and mark-on-surface pairing the app uses, the shared shell's and the mirror's (`tests/lib/prototype.mjs`), in both themes: 4.5:1 for text (1.4.3), 3:1 for marks, icons, control borders and the focus ring (1.4.11). The test fails below that. It also fails when the app's CSS or JavaScript names a Vici token that does not exist, or when a pairing names a token one theme lacks.

**Colour-blind separation.** The state colours, the network, checking, the comparison bars, merlot against danger, and the mirror's own pairs (a what-if against a contradiction, against checking and against the network at rest; the route today against the route as published), simulated for normal vision, protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia with the Machado et al. (2009) matrices at full severity in linear RGB. The former palette was generated to keep every pair at least 0.10 apart in OKLab; Vici's tones fall short of that in places, for example a contradiction and an approved re-publication (danger and success: 0.030 in the light theme, 0.020 in the dark one), a what-if and a reading (lilac and fjord: 0.022 and 0.011), and merlot text and danger (a hue gap of 16.7° and a lightness gap of 0.03, where the former palette kept 18° and 0.12). The test reports these gaps rather than failing on them, because they are Vici's to fix: the app makes up for each in form (§3: a contradiction carries a warning mark and a solid ring, an approved re-publication a check; a what-if a dashed ring and the sparkle, a reading a pulse and the word *Reading…*), and the colour-blind captures of `tests/design.mjs` show it.

## 6. Agentic patterns

Yocco's six patterns for agentic AI, and where each one lives:

| Pattern | In the Timetable Translator |
|---|---|
| Intent preview | The **progress rail** shows the agent's five-step plan before the run starts (Spot, Check, Calculate, Propose, Prepare), and the intro window explains it |
| Autonomy dial | Fixed at one level on purpose: *plan and propose, you approve*. Every outbound action (passenger message, re-publication order, override request, staff briefing, hold request) is a draft that needs its own approval |
| Explainable rationale | The recommendation line, *Confirmed:* and *Revised: old → new* with their evidence, the reasoning summaries, the AI label's explanation, and **Guardrail** notes when the code refuses a step |
| Confidence signal | Alerts are *verified* (two distinct sources, and for every train the source record, the published view and a corroborating physical observation from the run, enforced in code) or labelled *what-if*; the mirror's caption tag says whether a result is a first estimate (lilac, honey when it left channels, stops or trains out, which the impact line names), verified against the source (fjord), or a what-if; *Not confirmed* lines in honey |
| Action audit and undo | The Audit tab and its JSON export. Undo is not needed: nothing leaves the outbox |
| Escalation pathway | The agent hands over at each decision (the rail turns merlot), continues as a labelled what-if when it cannot confirm, and ends as **Stopped** rather than guess |

**Progress rail** (`js/progress.js`, on Vici's stepper), **handover** (`js/handover.js`), **AI labels** (`js/ai_label.js`), **guardrail notes** (`js/console.js`) and **discarded responses** work exactly as in the sister apps: the rail is ink while the agent works, with a pulsing hexagon on the current step, merlot with *Your decision* or *Your approval* when the run waits for a person, a tick in ink when done. The AI label (Vici's feature tag, the sparkle and **AI**) says who wrote the content (the live model by name, a recorded run, or the scripted replay), that every time, platform and passenger figure comes from the source and the deterministic sync engine and was checked against tool results, and what approval does. A draft whose facts could not be matched after two refusals carries a honey **Unchecked facts** tag.

**Evidence on the alert.** Below the sources the agent cites, a verified alert card lists what the run actually checked, from the tool results: *Checked in this run: operations log: OPS-05; published customer view: ICE 618; physical layer: PHY-01, PHY-02; source timetable: ICE 618*. The agent's words and the record of the checks stay apart.

**What the agent reads, live on the mirror.** A lookup pulses what it reads in fjord (the source column, a channel chip, a station), and its result leaves a fjord tag; a sim_sync call makes the plain lines run fjord while the engine compares. Both producers emit the same events, so a replay and a live run look alike.

## 7. Built for the room

- **Projection.** Muted colours blend on a projector (VUMC). Pastels therefore only ever fill; the meaning sits in a deeper stroke or text step at 3:1 or more. The options view is reviewed under a simulated washout (`contrast(0.62) brightness(1.18) saturate(0.8)`).
- **Emphasis.** One thing per moment: the recommended option has the merlot frame, its tag on the frame and the only solid Approve; the other options have soft or outline buttons; the rail highlights one step; on the mirror only the rows and cards of a finding carry colour.
- **Size.** The root font scales to about 17 px at 1920 px wide; **Larger text** enlarges the console. Vici's buttons, tabs, tags, menu items and fields keep the sizes the app had before Vici (§10). Timetable rows are 44 px high on a tall mirror (40 px when it is short), station names about 16 px, platforms about 18 px in bold. Every control is at least 40 × 40 px (`tests/layout.mjs`, `tests/smoke.mjs`): rows, channel chips, monitor rows, the strip's stations (a 46 × 40 px hexagon around each), the legend's toggles.
- **Nothing covers the finding.** The alignment view sizes itself by its own height (tall, mid, short, tiny): with the drawer open its channels sit two by two, then in one row with the verdict as a mark and the values one under the other; a verdict that does not fit beside its value moves under it, and no source value, channel value or verdict is cut to an ellipsis; centred while it fits, it never pushes its top out of view. The legend docks beside the views (below them on a narrow mirror) and never covers a row, the strip or the caption. When the drawer opens, or the docked legend takes the room, the mirror gives way in order: the strip goes first, then the card's source note, check tags and column titles (the channel chips keep one row where they can, and on a laptop leave their versions to the caption), and on the shortest mirror the head, so the timetable rows stay. After the room changes, the first contradiction is scrolled back into view. A card narrower than 760 px drops the Ril 100 code column (the strip and the popovers keep it) and the station names keep their room. The line labels of a link with two contradictions stack one under the other, never on top of each other. `tests/layout.mjs` checks all of it, cut words included, in five viewports and seven states, including the finding during the run, the options, the legend opened by the presenter and the monitor, and the other six scenarios at 1280 × 720 and 1600 × 900.
- **Focus.** Vici's focus ring: 2 px of `--vc-focus` with a 2 px gap, an outline on rectangles and a hexagonal ring on hexagon controls (the strip's stations, the channel chips, the legend's toggles, the alignment view's numbers, icon buttons). Rows with a state bar keep the ring next to it. The mirror draws its rows afresh on each event, and a row that has the keyboard focus gets it back after the redraw; a popover finds its row again after one; a monitor row opened by keyboard moves the focus into the card; Escape closes a popover or the legend and gives the focus back to what opened it. After a decision the options and the outbox rebuild their cards; focus moves to the next decision (the next Approve) instead of falling back to the page.
- **The scenarios in view.** Before a run the console lists every preset scenario, a 40 px row each with its hexagon number, its title and its kind of contradiction; scenario 1 carries *Start here*, and the one loaded is framed in 2 px of ink like a selection on the mirror, its number in merlot, the colour of **Start agent**, which runs it. On a short window the rows drop their second line and the plan's sentence, so all of them fit without scrolling at 1280 × 720. After a run, *Try another scenario* stands above the run's last line as outline buttons with the same numbers, in equal columns (two in a laptop's console, three on a large screen, a long name wrapping inside its button), so its line stays in view with the run's last line below it; the console still ends on the run's facts. The Scenario control reads *Scenario 1 of 7*, and on a laptop, where its caption hides, *1 of 7* before the name.
- **Motion.** One pulsing hexagon on the active rail step, the streaming cursor, and on the mirror: a fjord pulse on what the agent reads, plain lines that run fjord while the engine compares, a short flash on a row whose state changes. All of it stops under `prefers-reduced-motion`, and the mirror then shows its final state at once (`tests/smoke.mjs`, case *motion*).
- **Words.** Labels name what a person understands: *Your decision*, *Approve*, *Accept the risk*, *Forget key*; on the alignment view *The source*, *The translator*, *What passengers are told*, *Contradicts the source*, *Re-publication approved*; on the timetables *Betriebsfahrplan* and *Kundenfahrplan* with their English roles (*source*, *as published*), *Betriebshalt: no passengers*, *Durchfahrt*. Each tool call in the console is one plain sentence (*Reads what every channel tells passengers about ICE 618*); the tool's own name, its arguments and its result sit behind a closed **Details**, an alert's sources and checks behind **Sources and checks**, the summary's text behind **Read it here** and the run's facts behind **Replay facts** (**Run facts** live), so the console reads as a story and the record stays one click away. The status tag uses the words of the five steps (*Spotting*, *Checking*, *Calculating*, *Proposing*, *Preparing*, and *Assessing* while the agent works out who is affected), never *Simulating*, the word a Simulation run's badge already uses; the console writes a phase line only when the agent hands over (*Phase: Awaiting your decision*, *Phase: Awaiting your approval*); a platform is *platform 7* in English text and *Gleis 7* only in German, on the cards, the sensors and in the console alike. Option cards carry a short title, *All channels aligned*, *Kept by hand* only where values are kept by hand, one big figure, *Still misinformed*, and one short line on who that is (*Some already head for the old platform*); what the option does and the staff effort are in the card's tooltip. The do-nothing card has two rows (*First affected*, *Next cycle*). A draft in the Outbox shows what it is, for whom and its subject line; its text opens with **Show draft**. KPI tiles say what and when in a few words: *Misinformed passengers 312, verified against the source*; *First affected in 14 min, ICE 618, Köln Hbf 07:42*; after a decision *left with option A (was 312)* and *info in 2 min, aligned in 8 min* (the tooltip has the full sentence). The header keeps two badge rows down to 1280 px in every scenario (`tests/layout.mjs`): Inter and Bricolage run wider than the former fonts, so "How it works" sits beside the mode badge and keeps only its icon below 1300 px, the theme toggle sits beside it, and the tiles' second lines wrap at most once. The AI disclosure keeps its full sentence, "You are interacting with an AI system", at 1280 px and wider.

## 9. Changing things safely

**Keep the sister apps in step.** The Timetable Translator, Rebate Hunter, the Navigator and Repack Pilot share one shell (`REVIEW.md`, *Shared shell*): `vici/` (the same Vici release, unchanged), `css/` except `css/mirror.css` and `css/align.css` (each prototype's centrepiece has its own stylesheets) and the marked app block at the end of `css/style.css`, `js/theme.js` and the shell modules listed there, and four tests that are the same file in every prototype: `tests/tokens.mjs`, `tests/hexagon.mjs`, `tests/page-icon.mjs` and `tests/vici-files.mjs`. What differs between the prototypes in those tests (the colour pairs only one prototype draws, the tour the hexagon test takes, the page icon) lives in one place, `tests/lib/prototype.mjs`, and the shared tests use only what every prototype's `tests/lib/harness.mjs` exports. Change a shared file in all four repositories in the same way, or the prototypes drift apart; `tests/parity.mjs` compares this shell with Rebate Hunter's, and CI runs it against Rebate Hunter's `main`.

**Colours come from Vici.** There is no palette to regenerate any more. To use a colour, pick the semantic token whose meaning fits (§3, §4), never a family primitive or a value of your own, and add the pairing to the contrast test: to `SHELL_PAIRS` in `tests/tokens.mjs` for the shared shell, to `COLOUR_PAIRS` in `tests/lib/prototype.mjs` for the mirror. To change a colour, change Vici: a fix belongs in Vici's next release, which then comes here as described in `vici/README.md` (copy the files, change the version, run the tests, look at the prototype in light and dark). Never edit the copy in `vici/`. `favicon.svg` is the one file with colour values of its own: change it together with the website's card.

**Keep the grammar.**
- Merlot only for what a person does or decides, and never as a mark among state colours. A selection on the mirror is ink.
- State colours only for the state of passenger information, and always with a word, a mark, a line style or a shape.
- Fjord only for checking: what the agent reads and what a lookup confirmed.
- Lilac only for what the AI wrote, thinks or assumes. New AI-written content gets an AI label.
- No colour values in components: use a token. Anything round is a hexagon; anything pill-shaped a hex capsule.
- New interactive elements are at least 40 × 40 px and use Vici's focus ring, hexagonal on a hexagon; an element with its own shadow keeps the ring next to it.

## 10. Deviations from Vici

Each one deliberate, with its reason. The first come with the shared shell (Rebate Hunter's), the last are the mirror's own.

| Where | Vici | Timetable Translator | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Buttons, icon buttons, inputs, menu items, tabs | 2.5 rem controls with .875 rem text (37.5 px at a 15 px root); tabs .875 rem; menu items 2.25 rem | 44 px with 1 rem text; compact buttons 40 px with .875 rem; tabs and menu items 44 px with 1 rem | The room reads the screen from a distance, and nothing clickable is under 40 px (WCAG 2.5.8, projector use) |
| Tags and count badges | 1.5 rem with .75 rem text; badges 1.25 rem | Tags 26 px with .8125 rem (the status tag 44 px, the mode badge 36 px, the AI disclosure 36 px); badges 24 px | Today's sizes: the state words must read from the back of the room |
| Profile links in the byline | 24 px targets | 44 px | The mirror's and the bar's targets are 44 px |
| Field labels, help and alerts | `vc-label` .875 rem, `vc-help` .75 rem, alerts .875 rem | Labels 1 rem, help .875 rem, alerts 1 rem | The sizes the app had: they are read from the back of the room |
| Danger button | Solid danger fill | Abort and Forget key as an outline in the danger tone | A solid red bar in the control bar would shout at the room for an action that is rarely needed |
| Segmented control | `vc-segmented`, a radio group | The replay speed and the mirror's views stay groups of toggle buttons with `aria-pressed`, drawn like Vici's segmented control | A radio group would change the keyboard path and break the behaviour tests |
| Toggles the app owns | vici.js flips `aria-pressed` on `.vc-chip` and `.vc-button--toggle` | The legend's toggles, the view buttons, the channel chips and *Larger text* use neither class; the app's own code flips them | One owner per control, so a toggle never flips twice (`tests/smoke.mjs`, case *once*) |
| Dialogs | 85 vh high; vici.js closes a dialog on a click on its backdrop | Today's widths (880, 620, 1080 and 1000 px), the window's height less 48 px; the app owns Escape, the backdrop and the focus trap (`data-vc-dismissable="false"`) | The intro and the mode choice fit a 1280 × 720 projector without scrolling; a decision or a typed key is never dropped by a stray click |
| Menus | `.vc-menu[popover]`, fixed to the viewport by vici.js | `.vc-menu` without `popover`, opened under its toggle by the app | The control bar's dropdowns keep their keyboard paths and their place |
| Drawer tabs | `.vc-tabs` bound by vici.js | `.vc-tabs__list` without `.vc-tabs` | The app's tabs close the drawer when the open tab is clicked again |
| Audit table | `vc-table-wrap` scrolls sideways | It clips, and the drawer scrolls | The header row sticks while the audit scrolls with the drawer; its columns fit the drawer |
| The AI label's note | `vc-popover` with `popover`, placed by vici.js | The popover's look without the attribute, opened, placed and closed by `js/ai_label.js` | A toggletip that stays open until a click elsewhere or Escape and gives the focus back (Carbon for AI, Pickering) |
| Progress rail | Labels under the markers; `vc-step--done` in merlot | One line, each label beside its marker; done steps ticked in ink; the step that waits for you merlot with *Your decision* | The rail must not take height from the console; merlot means the person's turn, and a finished step is the agent's |
| Intro steps | Centred labels; Vici's current and done looks | Flush left; each step's hexagon in the tone of its moment (danger, fjord, honey, ink, success); on a short window the hexagon sits beside the step's title | The steps' hints read better flush left; the tones introduce the grammar; the intro fits 1280 × 720 without scrolling |
| Recommended option | A tag in the card's head | Its tag sits on the card's top frame | Inter runs wider than the former fonts: the cards still fit a 1080p drawer with the title on two lines |
| KPI tiles | `vc-stat` has no tones; figures 1.75–2.25 rem; the note in `--vc-text-subtle` | A tile that shows a state takes that state's tone; figures 2.125 rem, 1.875 rem from 1700 px down; the note in `--vc-text-muted` | The strip turns from danger and honey to success as the run moves; the header keeps its height; the note (*verified against the source*) must read on a projector |
| Mode markings | Sentence case | SIMULATION and LIVE in capitals | Tests and the downloaded files rely on them |
| Simulation | No tape in Vici | A tape of `--vc-text` and `--vc-surface` stripes with the word SIMULATION | A replay must never pass for a live run, and Vici's honey now means *at risk* |
| Disabled buttons | `pointer-events: none` | Pointer kept; the code ignores the click | The title still says why the button is off |
| Tooltips | Shown on hover and on any focus | On buttons with words, only while the words are hidden; not when a dialog moves the focus after a click | A tooltip that repeats the button's words, or pops up when a dialog opens, is noise |
| Scrollbars | The browser's | Thin, in the border tone, on the app's own scrolling panels (the console, the drawer, the mirror's three views, its legend and popovers) | Wide scrollbars take room from the console and the mirror on a 1280 px projector |
| The mirror's selection and toggles | Pressed controls in merlot (`--vc-accent-soft`, `--vc-accent`) | Ink: a 2 px `--vc-text` frame on the view shown, the channel shown and the train in focus, ink ticks on the legend's toggles | Merlot sits close to danger; never among state colours |
| The mirror's tags | 1.5 rem with .75 rem text | The timetables' check tags and the alignment view's tags 21 px, the channel tags of *All trains* 20 px, with .72–.74 rem | Today's sizes: they sit in the mirror's dense heads and rows, under the titles they qualify |
| Stations, end marks and swatches | `vc-cell`, `vc-hex-ring` (clip-path fills) | SVG hexagons, and clip-path rings of the app's own for the channel chips, line labels and spine stops | An outline that is dashed (a what-if, a stop only the published timetable has, a channel behind the source) needs SVG or a ring of its own |
| Rows, stations and chips without a tooltip | Icon-only buttons are named by a Vici tooltip through `aria-labelledby` | Each keeps its full name as `aria-label` (a station's name, code, role and state; a channel's version and contradictions), and a channel chip keeps the browser's title as its hover hint | The name says more than a tooltip should, and Vici's tooltip would replace it |
| The mirror's popovers | `vc-popover` with `popover`, placed by vici.js | The popover's look without the attribute, placed beside what opened it by `js/pop.js` | A popover follows its row or station across redraws, and Escape gives the focus back to it |
| Management summary | `vc-prose`: one column at the prose measure | The same column; on a screen of 1100 px and more the summary's table stands beside it, from its first paragraph down | The summaries run longer than Rebate Hunter's: this way they fit 1920 × 1080 without scrolling, as 1.2.0's did, at no more than 75 characters a line |
